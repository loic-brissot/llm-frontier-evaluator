    How it works. A 4 m terrain model of inner Paris is built from the median ground elevation of 127,000 Apur building footprints and 9,800 street levelling benchmarks (Ville de Paris, IGN69). The water surface is not flat: it follows the slope of the Seine, calibrated bridge by bridge on the 1910, 1955 and 1982 water lines and the lower-quay altitudes published by VNF. Each 4 m cell gets the gauge level at which water first reaches it by a continuous surface path from the river; buildings and stations take the lowest value of their footprint or surroundings.

    Limits. Surface overflow only: flooding through sewers, cellars, the metro and groundwater (a major factor in 1910) is not modelled, and neither are temporary defences or embankment parapets. Stations are flagged when water reaches the street around them, not their underground platforms. This is an educational visualisation, not an official hazard map — refer to the PPRI.
